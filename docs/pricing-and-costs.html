Because almost all the cost lives in the free tier, halving it ~4×s net profit (the paid side barely moves). Getting free-user cost to $0.25 is the single most important lever — see §6.

6.How to minimize cost

Ordered by impact. The first two get free-user cost from $0.50 → $0.25 and cut the largest variable line for everyone — they're where almost all the savings are.

LeverWhat to doImpact
1. Cap the free tier
biggest single lever		 Expire free-user clips after ~7 days (don't store forever), limit exports to 720p, drop to 3 auto-clips/mo, a ~2 GB storage quota, and a hard watermark. Free users shouldn't accrue permanent storage or heavy compute. $0.50 → ~$0.25
≈ 4× profit
2. Fix storage & bandwidth architecture Move clip storage off Vercel Blob to a zero-egress object store (Cloudflare R2) behind a CDN — egress is the silent killer. Never persist source VOD downloads: process and discard, keep only the final export. Compress exports. Cuts the top variable line for all tiers
3. Keep heavy editing client-side Trim/crop/caption/export already run in the browser via FFmpeg — near-zero server cost. Preserve that; resist moving rendering server-side as you add features. Keeps compute ≈ $0
4. Make the Auto-Clipper efficient Cache VOD scans & heatmaps, dedupe repeated scans of the same VOD, and download only the highlight sections (yt-dlp ranges) rather than whole VODs. Cuts compute + bandwidth
5. Trim AI-text spend Stay on the cheapest Gemini Flash tier, debounce/cache title & hashtag suggestions so a user can't spam calls, and keep AI text off the free plan. Already cents — keep it there
6. Push annual billing One 30¢ processing fee per year instead of per month, and annual plans cut churn sharply. +~$3/yr per payer, lower churn
Bottom line. Don't chase the paid-side costs — they're already 73–77% margin. Profit at this pricing is decided almost entirely by (a) how cheap a free user is and (b) the free-to-paid ratio. Levers 1 & 2 attack (a) directly; a better onboarding/upgrade flow attacks (b).
