AI Video Generation for Vybe

A credit-based AI video feature for the clip editor — generation model, pricing tiers, card & crypto payments, unit economics, first-year cost projection, and a hosting strategy beyond Vercel.

Prepared June 2026 · Status: pre-launch concept · Figures are estimates with stated assumptions and will move with provider pricing and real usage.

1 · The feature

Inside the existing clip editor, a “Generate” action lets a user create a short video from a text prompt (text → video) or by animating an uploaded image (image → video). The result drops straight into the editor, where every existing tool — trim, crop, captions, subtitles, music, export — works on it exactly like an imported clip.

Generation runs through a pluggable provider (already built): fal.ai / Kling by default for cost, with Google Veo available behind an environment switch. Cheap open models (Wan, LTX) can power lower tiers. Because each generation costs real money, access is metered with a per-user credit balance.

2 · Credit model

One currency — credits — filled by three taps and spent on generation. A credit is a unit of cost, not a unit of clips: pricier models burn more credits, so margin stays constant regardless of which model a user picks. The cost per generation is shown in the UI before they commit.

How credits are granted

  • Signup grant — one-time free credits to drive activation.
  • Subscription — monthly/annual plans refill credits on a monthly cycle (annual drips monthly, it does not dump a year at once).
  • Referral — small bonus to both sides on signup, larger bonus on the referee’s first payment.

Rules

  • Rollover, capped at 1× monthly allotment — unused credits carry over up to one month’s worth.
  • Deduct on success only — failed generations don’t burn credits (atomic UPDATE … WHERE credits ≥ cost).
  • Model access by tier — higher tiers unlock premium models and may freely choose which model to spend on.
  • Ledger-backed — every grant/spend/refund is an append-only row for auditability once money is involved.

4 · Payments — cards & crypto

Offer both rails. Cards cover friction-free recurring billing; crypto adds lower fees, global / unbanked reach, no chargebacks, and fits a crypto-friendly creator & streamer audience (Twitch / Kick).

The catch: crypto + subscriptions

Crypto has no native “auto-charge me every month.” The clean way to reconcile this with the credit model:

  • Cards → subscriptions — monthly / annual auto-renew, refills credits each cycle.
  • Crypto → one-time credit packs — e.g. “buy 200 credits for $39.” Fits the credit model perfectly: crypto users just top up their balance when low, no recurring charge needed.
  • Advanced (optional, skip for v1): stablecoin subscriptions via smart-contract allowances / payment streaming (x402-style) — emerging, more engineering.
Operational realities: settle in stablecoins (USDC/USDT) or use a processor that instant-converts, so price swings don’t eat margin. You still pay fal & hosting in USD — auto-convert at settlement or hold USDC and off-ramp. No chargebacks (a plus), but refunds are manual, and crypto adds KYC / AML + tax-accounting overhead that scales with volume — budget a little legal time.

Effect on costs

Processing fees fall from ~3% (cards) to ~0.5–1% (crypto) on whatever share settles in crypto. The dollar saving is modest next to AI COGS — crypto’s real value is reach and zero chargebacks, not the fee delta — but it’s real:

Fixed Year-1 total ≈ $600–1,200. Payment fees come out of revenue, not upfront — Stripe at 2.9% + $0.30, or crypto rails at ~0.5–1% (see §4). Development is ≈ $0 cash if built in-house with AI assistance ($10k–40k if outsourced).

Variable cost (COGS) — scales with usage, revenue-funded

The only uncovered variable cost is the free tier, which is why Free runs on the cheapest model:

  • Free user on Wan: ~3 clips/mo × $0.10 ≈ $0.30/mo (trivial).
  • Free user on Kling (if allowed): ~$1.05/mo — 3× worse. Hence Free = Wan only.
  • Paid user: credits priced so COGS ≈ 25–35% of revenue; the rest is gross margin.

What blows this up if ignored

1. Free-tier abuse — throwaway emails farming free credits. Mitigate with email verification, optionally a card on the free trial, and IP/device rate limits.
2. Storage creep — generated + uploaded videos pile up. Auto-expire AI clips after 7–14 days (fal deletes its copies in 7) to keep storage roughly flat.

Benefits beyond revenue

  • Differentiation — generation + editing + scheduling in one tool is a strong wedge.
  • Referral flywheel — credits are a cheap, native incentive (no cash payouts).
  • Upsell ladder — model gating gives a concrete reason to move Free → Creator → Studio.

7 · Hosting strategy (alternatives to Vercel)

Key architectural fact: heavy video encoding already runs client-side (ffmpeg.wasm in the browser), and AI generation runs on fal’s servers — not yours. Your servers only do light work (auth, API, short ffmpeg audio extraction, orchestration). That makes storage & bandwidth, not compute, the real scaling cost — which changes the hosting calculus.
OptionBest forCostTrade-off
VercelNext.js frontend + light API; fastest DX$20/mo + usage Function compute & Blob egress get pricey at scale; 300s limit
RailwayContainer apps + background workers (server ffmpeg)~$5–20+/mo usage Less edge presence; manage your own scaling
RenderPredictable per-service pricing, cron + workers$7+/mo per service Cold starts on cheap tiers
Fly.ioContainers near users, scale-to-zerousage, ~$5–30/mo More ops knowledge needed
AWS (ECS/Lambda + S3 + CloudFront)Max scale & lowest unit costcheapest at scale Highest complexity / setup time
Hetzner / DO VPSCheapest raw compute, full control€4–20/mo box You own all ops, patching, uptime
Cloudflare (Pages + R2 + Stream)Storage & video deliveryR2 = $0 egress Workers CPU limits — not for heavy ffmpeg

Recommendation — a pragmatic hybrid

  • Frontend + API: stay on Vercel early (best DX, you’re already there). Revisit only if function/bandwidth bills climb.
  • Storage: move generated/uploaded video off Vercel Blob to Cloudflare R2 (or S3). R2’s zero egress fees are the single highest-leverage cost change for a video product.
  • Heavy/long jobs (if you ever move encoding server-side): a small Railway/Render/Fly worker or a Hetzner box — far cheaper than Vercel function-seconds for long tasks.
  • AI generation: already offloaded to fal — no change needed.
Net: you don’t need to leave Vercel to launch. The one change worth making early is storage on R2/S3; everything else is a “revisit at scale” decision.
